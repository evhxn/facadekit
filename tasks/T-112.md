# T-112 – Quick re-align after the projector moved

- **Milestone:** v1
- **Recommended model:** Sonnet
- **Depends on:** T-027, T-032
- **Parallel with:** T-113, T-114, T-115

## Goal

When the projector has been bumped or re-mounted, let the user re-scan and have all surfaces move to the right place automatically, instead of redrawing them.

## Background

Each calibration stores `proj_view.png`, a photo of the house in projector pixel space. If the projector moves slightly, the new projector view is (approximately) the old one transformed by a single homography `H_drift` (exact for pure rotation about the lens; a good approximation for small shifts at house distances). We estimate `H_drift` by matching image features between the old and new projector views and apply it to every surface.

New route (prefix `/api/v1`): `POST /projects/{pid}/realign` with `{"old_calibration_id": str, "new_calibration_id": str, "apply": bool}` → `{"inliers": int, "reproj_error_px": number, "max_shift_px": number, "project": Project | null}`. With `apply: false` nothing is saved (`project` is the preview copy); with `apply: true` the transformed project is saved with `calibration_id = new_calibration_id` and returned. `max_shift_px` = the largest displacement of any surface corner.

## Steps

1. `estimate_drift`: convert both views to gray; apply CLAHE (`cv2.createCLAHE(2.0, (8, 8))`) to cope with night photos; detect `cv2.AKAZE_create()` features (use the measured masks, eroded by 15 px, as detection masks when given); match with `cv2.BFMatcher(cv2.NORM_HAMMING)` `knnMatch(k=2)` and Lowe ratio 0.75; `cv2.findHomography(old_pts, new_pts, cv2.RANSAC, 3.0)`. Fail with `REALIGN_FAILED` ("The two scans look too different to line up automatically.") when fewer than 30 inliers, median inlier reprojection error > 4 px, or the homography is implausible (maps the frame corners so that the area changes by more than ×2 or the quad becomes non-convex).
2. `apply_drift`: deep-copy the project; for each surface transform `quad` and mask points with `apply_h`. Mesh offsets are small relative displacements: transform each control point's *final* position (`base + offset`), recompute the base from the new quad, and store `new_offset = new_final − new_base`.
3. The clip timeline, assets and markers are untouched.
4. UI: on the Setup page, when the project already has a calibration and surfaces, the scan step offers "Projector moved? Re-scan and re-align". Flow: run a new scan → call `realign` with `apply: false` → show the new projector view with old surfaces (red, dashed) and transformed surfaces (green) overlaid, plus "Surfaces moved by up to N px" → buttons "Apply" (calls with `apply: true`, reloads the project) and "Keep new scan but place surfaces by hand" (just keeps the new calibration; the server already set `calibration_id` after the scan). On `REALIGN_FAILED` show the message and only the second option.
5. Remind the user after applying: "Check the outlines on the house and re-render the show."

Backend tests: build a textured 960×540 test image (random rectangles and text drawn with OpenCV so AKAZE finds features), warp it with a known homography (rotation 2°, shift (18, −11), slight perspective) → `estimate_drift` recovers it: transformed frame corners within 1.5 px of truth, `inliers >= 30`; two unrelated random-noise images → `REALIGN_FAILED`; `apply_drift` moves a quad's corners exactly by `apply_h` and leaves the timeline equal; mesh round trip: with the identity homography offsets are unchanged (±1e-6); route with `apply: false` does not change the stored project's revision, with `apply: true` it does and sets `calibration_id`.

Simulator test: scan `default_scene()`; scan again with a scene whose `proj_corners_world` are each shifted by (+12, −8) world px; the garage-door quad transformed by the estimated drift lies within 4 px of the second scene's `feature_quad_proj("garage_door")`.

Web tests: preview shows the "moved by up to" text from the mocked response; Apply calls `realign` with `apply: true`; `REALIGN_FAILED` hides Apply.

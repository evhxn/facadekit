## Goal

Run the calibration scan with the projector plugged into the Raspberry Pi instead of the laptop: FacadeKit uploads the stripe patterns to Falcon Player (FPP) as a video, FPP plays it, and the laptop's webcam records continuously. The recording is cut into per-pattern images by analysing brightness changes, so no network timing is needed.

## Why

The show is played by the Pi. If the Pi's HDMI output is scaled or offset differently from the laptop's, a laptop-driven scan is slightly wrong. Scanning through the Pi measures the real playback path and the user never moves the HDMI cable.

## Algorithm

`segment_recording`:

1. Brightness signal `b[t]` = mean of each recorded frame. Smooth lightly (median of 3).
2. Find the start marker: the pattern low, HIGH, low, HIGH, low, HIGH, low with roughly equal 1 s segments. Threshold at the midpoint between the 5th and 95th percentile of `b` over the first 15 s; find the first run of three HIGH segments each 0.6–1.4 s long separated by LOW segments 0.6–1.4 s long. The falling edge of the third HIGH (`t_a`) and the rising edge of the first HIGH (`t_0`) give the clock: one segment lasts `seg = (t_a − t_0) / 5`.
3. The lead-in's final black lasts one segment after `t_a`; pattern `k` occupies `[t_a + (1 + k)·seg, t_a + (2 + k)·seg)`. Use only the central 50 % of each window (skip the first and last 25 %) to stay clear of transitions, projector lag and camera exposure adaptation.
4. Sanity: pattern 0 of the sequence is `black` and pattern 1 is `white`; verify mean brightness in window 1 exceeds window 0 by at least half the marker's HIGH−LOW difference, else `SCAN_FAILED` ("Could not follow the pattern video. Make sure nothing else is playing on FPP.").

`record_and_capture`: lock camera controls as `run_capture` does (`set_controls(auto_exposure=False, auto_wb=False, autofocus=False)`), call `start_playback()`, then read frames for `total_seconds`, keeping for each a timestamp, a downscaled gray copy (width 160) for segmentation, and the full-resolution gray frame as `uint8` (38 patterns + lead-in ≈ 50 s at ≤ 30 fps is ≈ 1500 frames × 0.9 MB = too much RAM: **write full frames to a temporary `np.memmap`** or keep only every frame's gray at full resolution in a memmap on disk). After recording, segment, and for each pattern average the full-resolution frames inside its window into `images[k]`; `white_bgr` is taken from a colour frame saved at the middle of the white window (keep one colour frame per second in a small ring to find it).

Route: extend `POST /projects/{pid}/calibrations` with `mode: "scan_pi"` and required `host` (FPP address) and `camera_id`. Job: render the pattern video into the project's `renders/` folder as `rnd_<id>.mp4` (preset `pi3-1080p30`, silent audio), publish it as media `fk_<project_id>_scan.mp4` with playlist `FacadeKit - scan` and `start=False`; `record_and_capture` with `start_playback = lambda: FppClient(host).start_playlist("FacadeKit - scan")`; afterwards `FppClient(host).stop()`; then `run_calibration_from_capture`. The projector-size check uses the project's projector size (no projector window involved).

UI: Step 1 gets a choice "Projector is connected to: this computer / my Raspberry Pi (FPP)". Choosing the Pi asks for the FPP address (probe it), skips the projector-window requirement, and Step 3 starts `mode: "scan_pi"`. Text: "The scan takes about a minute. FPP will stop whatever it is playing."

## Pitfalls

- FPP takes an unknown time to start playback; the lead-in marker absorbs that. Record for `video length + 15 s`.
- Camera frame rate is irregular; always use timestamps, never frame counts.
- H.264 compression of 1-pixel-scale stripes: finest projected stripes are 8 px wide (`skip_lsb=2`) which survives CRF 20; do not lower quality for this video. Use `-tune stillimage`-like behaviour by adding `["-g", "30"]` (already in the preset) so each 1 s hold starts on a keyframe.
- Simulator: build a `SimPlaybackCamera` test helper that, given the rendered video and a start delay, returns the simulated camera image of the video frame that would be on screen at the (fake) clock time; advance the fake clock by 1/20 s per `read()` with ±10 ms jitter.
